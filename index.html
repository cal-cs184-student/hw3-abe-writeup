Part 1: Ray Generation and Scene Intersection

To figure out the ray, convert into camera coordinates, and then find the distance between the camera and the point on the plane, and then convert back into world coordinates. Now you have the ray. Then, using the ray, you find how long before the ray intersects by setting the shape equal to the ray and figuring out where it intersects the ray (the intersection closer to the camera if the object is a sphere).

I set the ray equal to the triangle or the sphere, and then I rearrange the equation so that you get an equal number of variables, and you get X, Y, and Z, for the three variables and then you solve for them. And then to solve for them, you use Cramer's rule, which will help you easily get the answer for the variables. Just remember to make sure its between max and min t with a simple check, and inside the traingle using the edges lengths.

Part 2: Bounding Volume Hierarchy

I build the BVH by basically splitting up the area by its midpoint. And then each midpoint each side of the midpoint checks if the ray intersects it, and if it does, then it'll check its smaller bounding boxes. Check the intersection using the same plane intersection from the previous question but changes a bit because the planes are not aligned to the axis. Use recursively smaller boxes by calling BVH on the smaller boxes until you get to a box size in which the number of leaves is less than the cap. Check for the nearest intersection and if you find a nearer intersection, overwrite it. The algorithm for splitting is very simple, it's just the midpoint, and then chekcing if the rays interesect either side of the midpoint and if so, continuing on the corresponding side(s).

On triangles of 50k, 100k, 134k, and 240k, the times with BVH stayed around .2 seconds, while without BVH, the times were 21, 53, 69, and 166 seconds correspondingly. This shows the speed-up power of BVH. By using it, and its power of being logarithmic, large increases in triangles barely make a dent in the generation time, because all that matters is the number of triangles seen. It goes from tens of thousands of tests per ray to less than 10, which adds up when checking through all the rays.
